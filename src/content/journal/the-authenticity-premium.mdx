Something interesting is happening in the data.

At the same moment that AI-generated content is flooding every platform, consumer trust in branded content is dropping. Ad fatigue is up. Engagement rates on polished, produced social content are declining. Meanwhile, raw, specific, clearly human storytelling is performing better than it has in years.

These two trends are not a coincidence. They are cause and effect.

## The flood and what it is doing

Generative AI has made content cheap to produce at scale. A marketing team that once needed a writer, a designer, and a video producer can now generate a week's worth of content in an afternoon. The volume of branded content online has increased dramatically as a result.

But volume without signal is noise. And audiences, who are remarkably good at filtering noise, are getting faster at recognizing AI-generated content and discounting it.

A 2026 Animoto study found that consumers are significantly more likely to trust video content they believe was made by a real person than content they suspect was generated or heavily automated. An EY report from the same year noted that authenticity is now among the top three factors driving consumer trust in brand communications, ranking above production value and creative originality.

Reuters Digital Media research found that audiences are increasingly skeptical of content that feels optimised rather than observed. The word they kept using in focus groups was "hollow."

Hollow. That is a useful word.

## What hollow looks like

Hollow content is technically correct. It hits the talking points. It uses the right keywords. It has clean production and a clear call to action. But it does not feel like it came from a specific human who observed a specific thing and had a genuine reaction to it.

AI-generated content is hollow almost by definition. It is trained on the average of human expression, which means it reliably produces the median version of whatever you ask it for. The median version of a brand story. The median version of a product explainer. The median version of an emotional appeal.

The median version of anything is forgettable.

What audiences are responding to instead is specificity. The detail that could only come from someone who was actually there. The sentence that doesn't quite sound like a press release because it wasn't written by a committee. The imperfect moment in a video that tells you the camera operator cared more about catching something real than getting a clean shot.

These things cannot be generated. They can only be observed.

## The premium

When something is scarce, it becomes valuable. Human-made content is becoming scarce in relative terms, even as the total volume of content increases. And scarcity, in content as in everything else, creates a premium.

This is already visible in how audiences engage with content. Documentary-style video, with its emphasis on observed rather than constructed reality, is consistently outperforming produced brand content in time-on-site and completion rates. Founder-led content, where a real person speaks directly from their own experience, is generating higher engagement than polished brand voice content on most platforms.

The production value arms race of the 2010s and early 2020s is over. What replaced it is not lo-fi content for its own sake. It is content where you can feel the specific human intelligence behind it.

## What this means in practice

For businesses commissioning content, the calculation has shifted. The question is no longer whether you can afford high-quality production. It is whether the content you are producing has a specific, credible human perspective at its centre.

A well-produced video featuring a real customer talking about a real problem they solved is worth more than a polished brand anthem built on stock footage and generated voiceover. A genuine founder story, told with specificity and some willingness to be imperfect, will outlast a hundred pieces of optimised content.

This does not mean production quality is irrelevant. It means production quality is table stakes. What creates the premium is the human truth underneath the production.

The brands and organizations that will win the next five years of content are the ones that understand they are not competing on volume or polish. They are competing on how real they are willing to be, and how well they can find the right collaborators to capture that reality.

That is a different brief than most marketing teams are used to writing.

But it is a much more interesting one.
